I'm an analog photographer, so I'm biased. Film is beautiful, and there's nothing quite like it. It's also expensive, and two minutes go by very fast. So here's the honest picture before you fall in love with the idea.

## What it costs

Rough numbers in reais, with dollars at about R$5.6 to the dollar in November 2026:

- **Super 8 film:** around R$400 (about $70) for a cartridge.
- **35mm film:** around R$600 (about $110) for roughly two minutes.
- **Development:** about the same again, around R$500 (about $90) for two minutes, or R$600 for a larger format.

Then think about what two minutes really gives you. Once you cut it, it's not that much footage. Plan your shots tightly.

It's worth it, though. It's really pretty.

## Where to develop it

There are local labs in São Paulo, so you can process in Brazil. That said, plenty of clients shoot here and take the film back to their reference lab in Europe, which is totally fine. You can send it there too. Use whoever you trust with your negatives.

If you're bringing a specific camera, bring it. Film cameras in Brazil are mostly a secondhand market. We wrote more about that in [Bringing Camera Gear to Brazil]({{ '/blog/bringing-camera-gear-to-brazil/' | relative_url }}).

## When I'd recommend film

When it adds to the texture and the style, and when it's connected to the brand and what it stands for. [Floyd]({{ '/work/floyd/' | relative_url }}) shot its whole campaign on 16mm on Ipanema beach, and the grain suits the brand's "bringing the mojo back" campaign. For [Nike × NOCTA]({{ '/work/nike-nocta/' | relative_url }}), the 16mm footage even became part of the campaign stills.

## When I wouldn't

If there's no real creative vision behind it, and you just want film because it's trendy, then it's just expensive. You'll pay for the look without it meaning anything.

In that case, there's a middle ground. Film emulation in post can mimic the look of film on digital footage. It's not the same, but it still works, and it costs a lot less.

## Our take

Film is a creative decision first and a budget line second. If it belongs in the story, it's worth every real. If it doesn't, save the money for a better location or another shoot day. We shoot 16mm, 8mm and large format, so if you're on the fence, talk it through with us.
