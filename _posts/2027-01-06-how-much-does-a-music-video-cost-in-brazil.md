You can make a music video in Brazil for around R$8,000. The question is what you'll get for it, and whether that's the video your song deserves.

Here's how the budget levels break down in practice, with dollars at about R$5.6 to the dollar (November 2026).

## The minimum: around R$8,000

About R$8,000 (roughly $1,430) is the floor for something good enough. It covers a good videographer, a location and some styling, and that's about it. It can work, but it's limited. There's not much room for more locations, more people or bigger light.

## The lower end of good: R$10,000 to R$12,000 for a day

Ideally you'd have more, and R$10,000 to R$12,000 (about $1,800 to $2,100) for a day of work is where a music video starts to have some breathing room. That's still the lower end, but it's a real shoot day with a proper team.

## A full production: at least $10,000

This is where it really depends on the creative vision. If you want people in it, several days, a really good cinema camera with lenses and operators, nice lighting, and both indoor and outdoor locations, plan for at least $10,000.

## What pushes the number up

- **Days.** One location in one day is the cheapest format. A three-day shoot across a theater, a beach, a favela and the artist's home, like one we did recently for Brazilian singer Winnie da Sul, is a different budget.
- **People.** Dancers, extras and casting real people all add up. Everyone gets paid properly.
- **Camera and light.** A cinema camera package with operators and lighting is a big step up from one videographer.
- **Styling and art direction.** We always have a stylist on a music video, and usually an art director. They make a huge part of the result, so they're worth keeping in the budget.

## Where you save

Sound, mostly. A music video usually doesn't need a sound recordist, which makes some things easier and cheaper. That changes if there are interludes with dialogue.
