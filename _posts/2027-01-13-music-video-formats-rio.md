"Music video" covers a lot of ground. It can be one room and a few props, or three days across the city with an art director and a crew. Picking the format first makes everything else easier, including the budget.

These are the formats we produce most in Rio, roughly from smallest to biggest.

## 1. One location, a few props

The short, focused version. One strong location, a few props, the artist and a good videographer. It's the most affordable format, and it can look great when the location does the heavy lifting, like a laje with a view, a bar with real character or a quiet beach at sunrise.

## 2. Vlog style

Loose, handheld and personal, following the artist around the city. We made two smaller, vlog-style videos for artists from Switzerland, and it's a great fit when the song is about a place or a moment.

## 3. Dancers

We can bring in dancers, and a video built around choreography can carry a lot of energy with a fairly simple setup: a location with space to move, a stylist and a good camera team.

## 4. Multi-location, multi-day

This is the full production. Recently we shot a three-day music video for a Brazilian singer across a theater, the beach, a favela and her home, with art direction tying it all together. It needs more people, more planning and a bigger budget, and it gives the artist a whole world on screen.

## 5. Travel

You can also go further. Brazil is huge, and a video can move from Rio's beaches to its rainforest, or out of the city entirely. We've scouted and shot in São Paulo, Minas Gerais and Maranhão, and the road itself can be part of the story.

## Picking yours

Start with the song and what the artist wants to say, then pick the format that serves it. If the message is intimate, one location might be stronger than five. If it's a celebration, give it dancers and a crowd.
