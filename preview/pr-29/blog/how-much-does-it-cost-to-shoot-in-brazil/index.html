How Much Does It Cost to Shoot in Brazil? A Rio Producer's Answer

November 23, 2026 · By Helene Doetsch

The question we get most isn’t about permits, safety or the weather. It’s “how much?”, and it usually arrives with a number already attached, one that was decided before anyone in Brazil was asked.

Here are real numbers, and what sits behind them.

The short answer

There’s no formal minimum. But if you’re shooting a full day with a few models and you want both photo and video, plan for at least $2,000 to $3,000. A typical smaller production, the kind most of our clients book, runs $10,000 to $20,000. Bigger productions, with several shoot days, a bigger crew and more locations, go up from there.

Next to the same shoot in Europe or the US, that’s cheap, by a lot. Next to what people picture when they think “a shoot in Rio”, it often isn’t, and most budget trouble lives in that gap.

Why Brazil costs more than you expect

Doing it properly has a price. A foreign production filming in Brazil has to run under a Brazilian production company registered with ANCINE, the national film agency. The city of Rio requires a permit to film in any public location, and that takes 7 to 15 working days. Every recognizable person on camera needs an image release that spells out where and for how long their face will be used. None of this is optional, and none of it is free. It’s also what makes your campaign safe to publish.

Good gear is an investment everywhere. We rent camera, lighting and grip from rental houses in Rio for each job, so you get the right kit rather than whatever we happen to own. Quality equipment costs real money wherever you shoot, and Brazil is no exception.

And we pay people properly. Models and talent in Brazil have professional rates, and crew deserve fair pay. It’s not ethical to underpay people, so we don’t build budgets that way.

What actually moves the number

Five things decide most of a budget:

  • Shoot days. A typical campaign for us is two to three days.
  • Crew. Our campaigns usually run with 20 to 25 people on set.
  • Talent. How many people, and whether you need models at the top of their careers through an agency, or real people cast from the street.
  • Locations. Beaches and Sugarloaf are straightforward. Christ the Redeemer has its own license process. Private villas and homes take longer to secure.
  • Deliverables. Our standard package is a hero film, shorts and reels for social, and photography. Shooting on 16mm or 8mm film adds stock, processing and scanning.

How we quote

You send a brief, a moodboard, or honestly just a date and a rough idea. We get on a short call about what you need and what you’re working with. Then we come back with a production plan: ideas, location and model options, and a budget that matches. There’s always room to be flexible on how we get there.

The quote is usually fixed. If something new gets requested on set, we add it. If the weather is truly bad, we move the date at no extra charge. We take payment in US dollars, euros, reais and other currencies, and Brazilian clients get a nota fiscal.

How to make the same budget go further

Shoot stills and motion with one crew instead of booking two separate productions. If you need a lookbook and a lifestyle campaign, do them in the same week, with the lookbook in a studio and the lifestyle out on location. Give us at least 14 days if you can, and more if you want public locations, because of the permit timeline. And if flying a whole team to Rio doesn’t add up, produce remotely. Soli&Sun directed its entire Rio campaign from Europe without anyone getting on a plane.

If you already have a number in mind, put it in the brief. It’s much easier to build the right shoot around a real budget than to guess one, and it’s the first thing we’ll ask about anyway.
