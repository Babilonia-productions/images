The Service

A Video That Sounds Like the Artist.

A music video is more personal than a campaign. A campaign is about the brand and its audience. A music video is about the artist: who they are, their personality, and the message they want to put out, whether that’s political, creative or a life lesson. Some subjects need to be handled with extra sensitivity, and we factor that in from the first call.

We work closely with the artist, the director representing them, and the label if there is one, so everything adds up to a video that represents the art.

How We Build It

Styling Always. Art Direction Too.

We always have a stylist on a music video, because it makes a huge part of the result, and usually an art director. On a campaign we sometimes skip art direction and let the local environment carry the look. A music video almost always needs someone thinking about the art.

Recently we produced a three-day music video for Brazilian singer Winnie da Sul, shot in a theater, on the beach, in a favela and at her home, with full art direction. We’ve produced videos for rappers too, including GGA Official from Tunisia, and two smaller, vlog-style videos for artists from Switzerland.

What We Handle

  • Concept development with the artist, director and label
  • Styling on every shoot, plus art direction
  • Dancers, real people and talent casting
  • Locations from beaches and favelas to theaters and homes
  • Cinema camera, lenses, lighting and operators
  • Behind-the-scenes content and stills from the same shoot

Common Questions
How much does a music video cost in Brazil?
The minimum is around R$8,000 (about $1,430), which covers a good videographer, a location and styling, but it's limited. R$10,000 to R$12,000 for a day is the lower end of where we'd like to be. With people, several days, a cinema camera, lenses, operators, good lighting and indoor and outdoor locations, plan for at least $10,000.
Do you need a sound recordist for a music video?
Usually not, which makes some things easier. If the video has interludes with dialogue, that changes.
Do you work with labels and directors?
Yes. We work with the artist, the director representing them, and the label if there is one, so the video adds up and represents the art.
Can you shoot in favelas and other hard-to-reach locations?
Yes. We shoot in favelas with locals who know the residents' association, and we've produced music videos across theaters, beaches, favelas and artists' own homes.
