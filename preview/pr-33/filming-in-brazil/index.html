PRODUCTION FAQ · UPDATED NOVEMBER 2026

Filming in Brazil, Answered.

Permits, paperwork, budgets and the questions crews ask before they land.

Shooting in Brazil is easier than its reputation, as long as someone local handles the rules. Below are straight answers to what brands, agencies and crews ask us most. For your specific shoot, email us and we'll walk you through it.

Regulatory answers reflect the rules as of November 2026. Sources: ANCINE, foreign filming authorization, ANCINE, Condecine FAQ (in Portuguese), and Rio Film Commission, filming permits.

ANCINE, Condecine and Paperwork
Do we need ANCINE approval to film in Brazil?
Yes, for a foreign production. ANCINE, Brazil's national film agency, requires a foreign shoot to run under a Brazilian production company registered with it, with a contract between the two. The Brazilian company files the shoot with ANCINE, which issues an official filming letter. Babilônia is registered with ANCINE and handles this filing for you.
Do we have to pay the Condecine tax?
Only if the work is shown in Brazil. Condecine is charged when a film or commercial is released or broadcast to the Brazilian public. If you shoot here and the work airs only abroad, there is no Condecine to pay. If it will also run in Brazil, we handle the registration, and because online and streaming placements are treated differently, we check the current rules against your media plan.
Is Babilônia registered with ANCINE?
Yes. We handle ANCINE registrations for both advertising and entertainment work as part of our legal services.
Can we bring our own equipment or ship products to Brazil?
Yes. Most productions rent gear locally, but when you bring equipment or ship products, we help with customs questions, imports and exports, and receiving packages, and make sure everything is legal and workable.
Is production insurance available?
Yes. In Brazil, insurance is arranged per production, and every production we run can be insured.
Permits and Image Rights
Do we need filming permits in Rio de Janeiro?
Yes. The city of Rio requires a permit to film in any public location. You apply online, and processing takes 7 to 15 working days. Beaches and natural parks are authorized by the city's Environment Secretariat, drones also need federal clearances, and Christ the Redeemer has its own license process. We handle all of it.
Do we need image releases for talent?
Yes, and in Brazil they have to be specific. A release sets how long the images can be used, where (social media and TV are different), and how talent can repost or use the work in their portfolio. Getting this right is one of the main reasons to work with a local partner.
Can we film anyone in public?
No. Brazil is strict about image rights, so people who are recognizable on camera need to give their consent, and locations need the right clearances. We handle both before the shoot.
Budgets, Payments and Timing
How much should we budget?
There is no formal minimum. A full shoot day with a few models and both photo and video needs at least $2,000 to $3,000. A typical smaller production runs $10,000 to $20,000, and bigger, multi-day productions run higher.
How do quotes work?
We quote based on your brief, and the quote is usually fixed. If you request something extra during the shoot, we add it.
How can we pay?
We accept international payments in US dollars, euros, Brazilian reais and other currencies, from clients everywhere from Australia to Indonesia. We send standard invoices, and Brazilian clients also receive a nota fiscal.
How much lead time do we need?
Plan for at least 14 days, and more if you're filming in public locations, because city permits take 7 to 15 working days. It also depends on how complex the brief is and how many products or setups there are. On private locations we can move faster when it's necessary.
Is producing in Brazil cheap?
Cheaper than Europe or the US, yes, but often more than people expect. Local rules have to be complied with, good gear costs money everywhere, and we pay crew, models and talent fair, professional rates.
Crew, Gear and Post
How big a crew can you handle?
Our largest crew so far was about 25 people, a mix of local and international.
Do you supply camera and lighting gear?
We have some kit for smaller productions, but we usually rent camera, lighting and grip from rental houses in Rio, so you get the right gear for each job.
Do you offer post-production?
Yes. We coordinate post-production with editors we work with regularly, chosen for each project.
Safety, Weather and Where We Shoot
Is it safe to shoot in Rio's favelas?
We work with local security and a local point of contact, and we monitor the situation before and during the shoot, including any concerns from residents or about public security.
What happens if it rains?
Sometimes rain suits the shoot. If the weather is really bad, we move the date at no extra charge.
Where in Brazil have you shot?
Across Rio de Janeiro, from the South Zone beaches to Grumari and favelas such as Cidade de Deus and Tijuquinha, plus Niterói, São Paulo, Minas Gerais and Maranhão. Next on our list: Amazonas and Goiás.
