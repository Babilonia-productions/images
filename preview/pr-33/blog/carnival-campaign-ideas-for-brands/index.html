Carnival Campaign Ideas: How Brands Can Do More Than Show Up

January 20, 2027 · By Helene Doetsch

Every year, a lot of brands show up for Carnival. The ones that do it well do it organically. Brands like Matte Leão, Baer-Mate and Heineken each bring their own local spin. Internationally, Jaded London is one we think did it really well.

But there’s a bigger opportunity most brands haven’t touched yet, and it’s the one that gets me most excited.

Go into the roots

Carnival didn’t come from nowhere. There’s so much ancestry and history behind it, in samba and in Brazil itself, and not a lot of brands have explored that yet. A campaign that connects to where Carnival comes from has something the usual glitter-and-crowds content doesn’t: a story worth telling.

It needs care. History and heritage are subjects to handle with sensitivity, and the people whose culture it is should be part of making it, not just in front of the camera. We’ve written about why that matters when shooting in Rio’s communities.

Collaborate with Brazilian talent

One of the strongest ways in is a collaboration. Partner with a Brazilian brand, or ask a Brazilian designer to adapt your looks for Carnival. If design isn’t the right fit, a Brazilian stylist can do it.

That turns a campaign from “we came to Rio” into something made with Rio.

Shoot the editorial, then join the party

Here’s the format I keep imagining. Start with an editorial shoot, the styled, considered version, with your Carnival looks and a proper photo crew. Then take the same people into a bloco and let the energy take over.

You come away with both: a classic campaign shoot and the raw, real Carnival content around it. Shot well, it could be really, really impactful.

Plan it like a production

None of this works if you improvise. Carnival is the busiest week of the year, and transport, crowds and security all need planning. Our Carnival shooting guide has the dates and logistics.

Our take

The campaigns people remember tell the story behind Carnival, and they’re made with Brazilian collaborators. If you want to build something like that for 2027 or 2028, talk to us early. We can connect you with samba schools, dancers, designers and stylists, and plan the whole thing around the city’s busiest days.
