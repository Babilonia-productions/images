Is It Safe to Shoot in Rio? How We Look After Crews and Gear

December 7, 2026 · By Helene Doetsch

A friend of ours needed to move gear across Rio for a shoot, so they booked a motorbike through an app because it was cheap and fast. The gear never arrived. The entire kit was stolen.

That story isn’t unusual, and it’s the clearest way we know to answer the question we get all the time: is it safe to shoot in Rio? Yes, if you run the shoot like someone who knows the city. Most of the bad stories start with someone trying to save a little money in the wrong place.

Don’t save $50 on the wrong thing

Sometimes it’s worth paying the $50 to get people you can trust. A driver we know, a lockable car, a local who walks with you into a neighborhood. Next to the value of a camera package, or a shoot day lost, that’s nothing.

We’ve built up trusted people in every place we work, so nobody gets lost and nobody walks into a sensitive area without security alongside them. And because we know the city, we know where problems tend to come up and where they don’t.

Our routine on a shoot day

It’s not complicated, and it works:

  • Someone always watches the gear, and that’s their whole job.
  • A separate security person watches the shoot, and on bigger productions there’s more than one, so the team can split up between locations.
  • The car locks. Our transport is a lockable vehicle, so gear that isn’t in use stays inside, not on the pavement.
  • There’s a local point of contact, and we keep an eye on what’s going on before and during the shoot, including any concerns from residents or about public security.

What a crew should never do

Never enter a favela, or just drive through anywhere, without understanding where you are. Even a road trip needs local knowledge. Driving from Rio to Búzios, for example, there can be safety issues along the way that a visitor would never spot.

And never go into a favela to film without a local. That means someone who knows the residents’ association and the people, plus the right permission. This is the rule we’re strictest about, and we wrote more about doing it properly in Shooting in the Favela.

When it goes wrong for someone else

We’ve been on location when another crew didn’t follow those rules, and we stepped in because we happened to be there. We’ve also had clients who got nervous the second favelas came up, and we could explain exactly what was and wasn’t a good idea. Most of what keeps a shoot safe is knowing which questions to ask before you go.

We love working in Rio. Respect the city, bring people who know it, and the footage is what you’ll end up talking about afterwards.

More practical answers, including insurance and what happens if it rains, are in our Filming in Brazil FAQ.
