Bringing Camera Gear to Brazil: What to Pack and What to Rent

December 14, 2026 · By Helene Doetsch

Bring the camera you love. Rent the tripod. That’s most of the answer to “should we bring our own gear to Brazil?”, but the details matter, especially if you shoot on film.

Bring what makes your look

Pack the camera gear you’re familiar with and like working with. Some things might not be available here, and the middle of a shoot day in Rio is not when you want to be learning a new camera.

The way we put it to clients: split your kit into two piles. In one, everything that makes your look unique, the camera body, the lenses, the specific glass you always reach for. In the other, everything that’s easily replaceable. Bring the first pile. Rent the second.

Everything’s possible, by the way. We’ve had people fly in with big, expensive cinema rigs, and it worked out fine.

Rent the rest in Rio

Lights, stands, tripods, grip and all the extra bits, we have everything here to rent, and it saves you a lot of luggage. We rent from rental houses in Rio for each job, so you get the right kit for that shoot rather than whatever happens to be lying around.

You can rent plenty of cameras here too, if you’d rather travel light.

The exception: film

If you shoot on film, think harder. 16mm cameras in Brazil are mostly a secondhand market, so what’s available at any given time is limited. If a specific camera is important to the look, bring it rather than hoping one turns up. (We shoot a lot of film ourselves, 16mm, 8mm and large format, so ask us what we can source before you decide.)

Customs and getting it here

If you bring equipment or ship products ahead of the shoot, we help with the customs questions, the import and export side, and receiving packages, and we make sure it’s all legal and workable. Rules for bringing in professional equipment have changed over the years, so check the current process with us before you pack, not at the airport.

Every production can also be insured, which is worth doing when you’re traveling with expensive kit.

Once it’s here

Gear is safest when it never leaves trusted hands. Don’t move it around the city on app motorbikes or leave it in an open car. We’ve written about exactly how we look after crews and gear in Rio, and it starts with someone whose only job is watching the equipment.

More on customs, insurance and lead times is in our Filming in Brazil FAQ.
