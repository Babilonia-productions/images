Shooting During Carnival in Rio: A Producer's Guide for 2027

January 18, 2027 · By Helene Doetsch

Carnival is the best time of the year in Rio. It’s so vibrant, and there’s so much going on that you could shoot for a week and only scratch the surface. It’s also when the city is at its busiest, and that’s what catches brands out.

Here’s how to produce a shoot during Carnival without losing your day to a crowd or a traffic jam.

The dates for 2027

  • Street blocos start on January 16.
  • Série Ouro parades at the Sapucaí run February 5 and 6.
  • Grupo Especial parades run February 7, 8 and 9.
  • Ash Wednesday is February 10.
  • The Champions’ Parade is on February 13.

The weeks before Carnival count too. Rehearsals and blocos are already out in the streets, and some of the best material happens before the main days.

Two ways to shoot it

You can go with the official blocos and see what happens, or you can set something up. We can connect you with samba schools and individual dancers and arrange shoots with them, which gives you control the street never will.

Either way, plan it. Even “let’s just go to a bloco and film” needs a plan for where, when and how to get there and out again.

What brands get wrong

Mostly, they don’t understand how busy it gets. Streets close, crowds build fast and plans that look simple on paper fall apart.

So choose the day, the time and the place together with your local production company, so you don’t run into crowds or problems on the way. Some days and neighborhoods are packed while others are far calmer, and a local team knows which is which.

Security isn’t optional

You need someone looking out for you. Shooting Carnival without a security team isn’t a good idea. Crowds, gear and excitement are a combination you want covered, so budget for security from the start. We wrote about how we look after crews and gear in Rio, and during Carnival all of it matters more.

Transport and hotels

Transport is really hard during Carnival. Getting from one location to another can take far longer than usual, so plan routes and timing ahead and keep the number of moves in a day low.

Prices go up too, for hotels and pretty much everything else. Book accommodation early and expect it to cost more than usual.

Permits and drones

Permits can actually be a little easier, because everyone’s shooting already. If you want something specific, though, it still needs to be arranged in advance. Our post on filming permits in Rio covers the basics.

If you want aerials, check early. Drones need their usual clearances, and temporary flight restrictions are issued during Carnival. More in Flying a Drone in Brazil.

Our take

Come for Carnival, but don’t improvise it. Pick the moments you want, plan the logistics around them, and bring people who know the city on its busiest days. If you’re thinking about what to actually make, our next post has ideas for brands.
