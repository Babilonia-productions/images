Fixer, Production Service or Production Company: Who Do You Need to Shoot in Brazil?

November 25, 2026 · By Helene Doetsch

This week we’re helping a photographer shoot at the horse races. It’s a small fixer job: a bit of access, a bit of local knowledge, and someone who knows where to stand. Other weeks we’re running a campaign with 20-plus people on set, casting, locations, permits and all.

It’s the same company doing very different jobs, and people ask us all the time which one they need. Here’s how we explain it.

First, the rule that applies to every foreign film shoot

If you’re a foreign production shooting film or video in Brazil, ANCINE, the national film agency, requires a Brazilian production company registered with it to be responsible for the shoot, with a contract between the two of you. That company files the shoot with ANCINE, which issues an official filming letter.

So whichever option you pick below, a registered Brazilian company has to be on the paperwork for a film or commercial. We’re registered, and we handle that filing.

A fixer: one person who knows the ground

A fixer is the right call when it’s you, a camera and maybe one assistant. Think of a photographer who’s bringing their own product, wants to hire one model and hit a few locations, and has some questions, a few security worries, or no Portuguese.

We fix for people outside audiovisual production too: travelers who need something specific, influencers, content creators and artists.

What a good fixer gives you is knowledge you can’t google: the streets, the people, who to ask for access, and how long the move between two places really takes. Our team is multilingual, so language is rarely an issue. What a fixer isn’t is a production. If you need a crew, casting and permits running in parallel, you’ve outgrown one.

If you need creative direction, a photographer, or someone to shape the overall project, you’ve moved past a fixer. That’s even more true if you’re making a film rather than just taking photos.

Production service: you bring the creative, we bring everything else

This is for agencies and brands arriving with their own director or creative team. You own the idea. We handle the ground: crew, casting, locations and access, permits, transport and airport transfers, catering, hotels if you want us to, and the problems nobody planned for.

Most of our production-service clients come from Europe and the US. We’ve done this alongside Mawe Productions on DSQUARED2’s SS26 campaign, shot at night in the Babilônia favela, and with executive producer Eden.br on Nike × NOCTA, where we handled talent scouting and location production.

Full production: from the idea to the final file

This is for brands who want the idea and the execution. We start with a short call, then come back with a pitch and production plan: ideas, location and model options, and a budget. A typical campaign is two to three shoot days with 20 to 25 people, and it delivers a hero film, shorts and reels for social, and photography.

It can move fast. Our NFL × Flamengo capsule for ProStandard went from the first conversation to the camera rolling in seven days.

So which one do you need?

Think about what you’re bringing. If it’s just you and a camera, a fixer will do. If you’ve got the creative team and need everything around it, that’s production service. And if you need the idea too, you want a production company. When in doubt, tell us what you’re planning and we’ll tell you honestly which one fits, even when it’s the smallest one.

The three mistakes we see most

The first is thinking you only need a fixer. We see this a lot: someone arrives with very little idea, creative direction or organization, and hopes a fixer can sort all of that out too. A fixer can’t. What they actually need is a production company.

The second is assuming you can turn up and shoot anywhere. Rio requires a permit to film in public locations, recognizable people need to give consent, and Brazil is strict about image rights. Sort it before the shoot, not on the day.

The third is budgeting for “cheap Brazil”. Shooting here is much cheaper than in Europe or the US, but usually more than people expect. We broke down what a shoot in Brazil actually costs earlier this week.

The rest of the practical stuff, from ANCINE to insurance to rain days, is in our Filming in Brazil FAQ.
