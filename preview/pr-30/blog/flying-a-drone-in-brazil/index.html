Flying a Drone in Brazil: What Foreign Crews Need to Know in 2026

December 16, 2026 · By Helene Doetsch

Yes, you can bring your own drone to Brazil. We also have drones of our own. Either way, a drone shot here involves three government agencies and an authorization for every single flight, and the rules changed in 2026, so a lot of what’s online is out of date.

This is how it works now. Drone rules move fast, so confirm the details with us before you fly.

Three agencies, three jobs

The Rio Film Commission sums it up: a drone shoot needs sign-off from ANAC, ANATEL and DECEA.

  • ANAC is the civil aviation agency. It registers drones and sets the rules for operating them.
  • ANATEL is the telecom agency. A drone’s radio has to be certified for use in Brazil.
  • DECEA controls the airspace. It authorizes each flight through its SARPAS system.

What changed in 2026

On June 16, 2026, ANAC’s new drone regulation, RBAC 100, replaced the old RBAC-E 94. Registrations and authorizations issued under the old rules stay valid until ANAC cancels them. DECEA also updated its airspace rules, in force from July 1, 2026.

What that means for your drone

  • Register it. Drones of 250 grams or more need to be registered with ANAC in SISANT. Guides disagree on whether lighter drones now need it too, because SARPAS only recognizes aircraft that are synced with ANAC, so check yours.
  • Certify the radio. The drone’s transmitter needs ANATEL certification.
  • Get insured. Third-party insurance applies under the new rules.
  • Request every flight. Each flight needs an airspace authorization from DECEA through SARPAS.

For foreign visitors, one 2026 guide says you can register with a passport. That comes from a single source, so it’s one of the things we confirm case by case.

Where you can’t fly

You can’t fly anywhere close to an airport or inside an airport zone. In Rio that matters a lot more than people expect, because Santos Dumont airport sits right in the middle of the city, next to some of the most famous views. Tell us where you want to fly and we’ll help you work out what’s possible. One guide also notes that DECEA issues temporary flight restrictions during Carnival.

Our take

If aerials are central to the idea, it can be easier to use one of our drones and a local operator who already has the registrations in place. If you’re bringing your own, send us the model and your locations early, and we’ll work through the paperwork with you.

Permits for the ground are a separate story. We covered those in Do You Need a Permit to Film in Rio?
