Making a Music Video in Rio: Why It's Not Just a Campaign With a Song

January 11, 2027 · By Helene Doetsch

We produce campaigns and music videos with a lot of the same people, the same gear and the same city. They’re still very different jobs, and it helps to know why before you start planning one.

It’s about the artist

A campaign is about the brand and its audience. A music video is more collaborative, with the artists themselves, their personality and who they are. It’s personal, and it usually carries a message. That message can be political, creative, a life lesson, or something else entirely, and it shapes every decision from casting to locations.

Some subjects need more sensitivity than a product shoot ever does. We factor that in from the first conversation, especially when the video goes into communities or touches on real lives.

Everyone has to agree on the art

On a campaign there’s a brief and a brand team. On a music video there’s the artist, often a director representing them, and sometimes a label. Our job is to work with all three so it adds up to a video that represents the art, not just a nice-looking shoot.

Styling always, art direction usually

We always have a stylist on a music video, because it makes a huge part of the result. And we usually bring in an art director.

That’s one of the clearest differences from our campaigns. For a campaign, we often skip the art director, because we want the local environment to carry the look: the real street, the real beach, the real laje. A music video almost always needs someone thinking about the art.

Sound is easier

Here’s one thing that’s simpler. A music video usually doesn’t need a sound recordist, because the track is already done. That frees up the set and the budget. The exception is interludes, where someone speaks, and then we plan for sound like any other film.

What that looks like in practice

Recently we produced a three-day music video for a Brazilian singer. We shot in a theater, on the beach, in a favela and at her home, with full art direction, so every location felt like part of her world.

We’ve also worked with rappers, including one from Tunisia, and made two smaller, vlog-style videos for artists from Switzerland. And for an actor based in France who wanted to reconnect with Brazil, we found a real Brazilian family of ten for his video. We told that story in How to Cast Real People in Brazil.

Our take

Start with who the artist is and what they want to say. Everything else, the locations, the styling, the budget, follows from that. If you’re working out the money side, here’s what a music video costs in Brazil.
